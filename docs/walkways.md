# Pedestrian surfaces and facilities

Ground footways, paths, pedestrian streets, cycleways, bridleways and tracks retain
the shared OSM road geometry. Their paving uses `walkwaySurface`; bridges retain
the existing deck and clearance planner. Explicit surfaces can change a footbridge's
appearance without changing its standing datum. Untagged engineered decks keep
their asphalt fallback. Underground pedestrian routing and station entrances stay
owned by `pedestrian.js`.

## Evidence and interpretation

OSM [sidewalk tags](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:sidewalk) retain their
forward-way left/right orientation. Side-specific tags outrank `sidewalk:both` and
`sidewalk`. `no`, `none`, `separate` and in-carriageway pedestrian lanes omit an
additional verge. Widths use real metres divided by the shared projection scale;
invalid explicit widths omit their side. Highway and access restrictions retain
precedence over appearance. Separately mapped footways receive their own paving,
without spawning another sidewalk around themselves.

Where sidewalk tags are absent, the room's satellite/OSM habitat observation must
support built cover beside an existing road. Gray satellite pixels alone cannot
create that built label. Exact semantic masks, including small holes, and settled
water/slope/occupancy gates bound the entire strip. A conservative enclosing disk
rejects holes between the surface probe stations. Missing observations omit
inferred sidewalks; explicitly tagged dry sidewalks can survive missing RGB.

[Surface tags](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:surface) outrank procedural
appearance. The catalog supports asphalt, concrete, paving stones, bricks, tiles,
setts, cobbles, flagstones, gravel, compacted earth, ground, sand, wooden planks,
metal grating and grass pavers. `paved` and `unpaved` specify a class, not a surveyed
material. Unknown surfaces use a habitat-compatible visual prior. The existing
old-street, cycleway and promenade themes retain bounded paving palettes in this
catalog instead of a private generic-gravel table in the road renderer; explicit
materials must have one owner to avoid contradictory defaults. Satellite cells
do not identify individual bricks, boards, rails or facilities. Appearance seeds
remain coherent within each mapped way and consume none of the shared scatter
sequence. No independent path centerlines are fabricated from coarse imagery.

## Ownership and placement

| Seam | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `walkwayCatalog.js` | Material appearances, contextual furniture choices and bounded detail envelopes. |
| `walkway.js` | OSM side/width/surface precedence and mapped facility classification. |
| `habitat.js` | Complete sidewalk strips, mapped ground-path anchors and coordinate-seeded furniture siting. |
| `habitatRender.js` | Footprint reservation before ground infill and bounded furniture instances. |
| `walkwayRender.js` | Terrain-clipped paving, fitted kerbs/rails/drains/tactile strips, authored furniture and texture ownership. |
| `walkwayMeshData.js` | Blender-baked metre-space members; generated by the matching authoring script. |
| `biomes.js` | Existing road geometry, bridge/tunnel datums and production integration. |
| `osmQuery.js` | Bounded point queries, shared routing and cache invalidation. |

Mapped benches, bins, bicycle racks, drinking fountains, shelters, picnic tables,
information boards, guideposts and bollards retain their coordinates if their
complete envelopes fit. Mapped points precede inferred dressing. Urban shoulders,
park paths and natural trails use different procedural furniture pools. Every
object stays outside the through corridor and yields to roads, buildings, reserved
facilities, semantic boundaries, water, unavailable heights and steep ground.
Benches and planters reuse the existing model seam. Mapped lamps, trees, signals
and street signs keep their existing owners.

Hard sidewalks can receive low chamfered kerbs and sparse schematic drains.
Lowered/flush/absent kerbs stay open. Tactile strips and ground handrails require
explicit tags. All new strips and furniture are presentation-only and do not
change HP, movement, standing, blockers, road grades or botanical collision.
Low-power mode keeps a prefix of the same plans. Materials release their private
textures when the existing battle teardown disposes them.

## Review boundary

The authoring script creates a separate editable Blender member library. The
browser review executes production builders for every material and urban, park,
forest and facility fixtures, exports their destination meshes, and uses the
shared Blender road-review assembler. The saved review packs texture images.
Synthetic fixtures establish rendering, deterministic replay, budgets and resource
release; they do not certify geographic reconstruction accuracy.
