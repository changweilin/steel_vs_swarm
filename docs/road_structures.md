# Road structures

`roadFurniturePlan.js` consumes built surface runs, the room's projected OSM points and closed island/tree-row ways, and the installed satellite observation field. [Mapped island outlines](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:area:highway%3Dtraffic_island) retain their footprints; crossing refuge nodes use fitted presentation geometry with a flush pedestrian gap. Broad median inference requires named or referenced opposing one-way carriageways on the same layer, sufficient separation to resolve an observation cell, and corroborating RGB vegetation probes. Narrow unresolved gaps, adjacent streets, water, invalid rings and unavailable heights omit islands. Returned arrays retain positions and source labels; their length counts accepted footprints rather than certifying surveyed inventory.

Individual tree and [street-lamp nodes](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:highway%3Dstreet_lamp) provide mapped positions. Tree-row ways provide a mapped corridor with schematic spacing. A [lit road](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:lit) does not identify pole positions, so the renderer does not invent lamps from that tag or from road width. Traffic controls require mapped signal evidence; heads face incoming arms, exclude outbound-only approaches and show a static, mutually exclusive intersection phase. Signs retain `roadSigns.js` and the existing text atlas. Satellite cells cannot identify individual poles, signs or tree species; botanical appearance remains owned by the existing forest seam.

`roadJunctions.js` fits balanced zebra stripes and approach stop lines to each arm. Paint omits motorway crossings and incomplete short approaches; intermediate collinear OSM vertices do not truncate the usable throat. `roadFurnitureRender.js` drapes island surfaces onto the terrain triangles and fits Blender-baked chamfered kerbs, signal housings, lenses and open hoods. Island tips receive bounded channelization hatches. Kerbs stay low and outside movement authority; legacy roadside candidate streams retain their RNG cost so changing evidence cannot shift later shared scatter. Habitat street dressing supplies benches and planters; mapped lamp placement has one owner. Editable review scenes retain their evidence arrays as Blender scene properties. Synthetic review cases demonstrate rendering and omission behavior, not geographic survey accuracy.

`public/js/roadStructures.js` owns bridge grades, connected-layer datums, support clearance, fitted roundabout islands and military-platform approaches. Bridge grade limits come from `MAPGEO`; impossible profiles omit their geometry and standing indexes together and count in `bridgeGradeRejected`. Underpasses and road culverts retain the existing tunnel planning and excavation seam.

`public/js/roadStructureRender.js` consumes normalized members baked by Blender from `tools/road_structure_authoring/author.py`. Full datum faces remain intact; concrete chamfers retreat into existing collision envelopes. Jersey barriers, portal frames, gallery columns, cap beams and platform slabs share that library. Expansion joints, drains and island curbs use bounded batches without shared layout randomness.

`roadJunctions.js` fits the Blender-baked corner profile to T, cross, Y, skew and multi-arm junctions. Boundaries use polygon triangulation because an unequal acute arm can hide the node behind its edge. Two-arm bends use continuous ribbons with shared tangents, widths and midpoints across OSM way seams. Adaptive angular sampling and radius-limited inner offsets prevent folded faces at tight bends; nearby nodes bound the curve reach. Width changes settle on straight approaches so the bend's inner edge cannot hook backwards. Paint consumes the same fitted profile. Surface nodes retain their OSM layer. Presentation curves and tapers never enter structural indexes; water-crossing decks retain the grading planner's path. Shared-RNG roadside placement keeps the original sampling path and run length.

`roadLaneEvidence.js` estimates missing lane counts from consistent raw satellite cross-sections with sufficient pixel resolution and two visible pavement edges. Explicit OSM lane counts, including paired directional counts, take precedence. `terrain.roadImagerySampler` shares the existing imagery provider and complete-tile cache with bounded requests and timeouts. Ambiguous, short, obscured or unavailable samples retain the highway-width fallback. Estimates remain separate from OSM tags and freeze in the host's raw road relay before pruning and quantization, so room clients do not repeat the image analysis.

`roadSigns.js` plans bounded roadside plates from the built dry runs, explicit traffic nodes and named OSM features. Street names attach to their own way; destination text retains forward/backward tags and numeric speed limits retain mph. Native names use the existing `vernacular.pickName` seam without translation. Tourist and civic guide arrows indicate the feature's bearing, not a computed driving route. Unknown sign codes, implicit speeds, missing names, unsuitable sites and unavailable glyphs are omitted. Sign-node queries request bodies because [Overpass tags-only output omits coordinates](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API/Overpass_QL#out). New feature queries invalidate the existing cache through `OSM_FEATURE_QUERY_VERSION`.

All plates share `worldtext.js` and its single atlas. Circular, triangular and octagonal plates use matching geometry and canvas outlines; sign posts and brackets consume Blender members. Glyphs with a missing-square advance are checked by raster shape so real fixed-width CJK names survive. Atlas overflow cannot enlarge the texture beyond its budget.

`laneGuidancePlan.js` consumes the NPC/minimap surface profile from `game._buildLaneSurf`; it does not march another height field or alter routes. Fixed reflector posts, approach-facing numbered route plates and hooded LED arrow boards replace the moving lane ribbons. Roadside offsets consume the existing road-sign segment seam and fitted widths; bridge posts stay inside their deck and tunnel plates narrow to fit the barrel. Invalid surfaces, insufficient headroom and base footprints omit furniture. Route text is owned by `laneGuidanceText.js`; a shared purple-and-amber legend, mother-lane number and lane color band distinguish these plates from ordinary green street signs without requiring color perception alone.

`laneGuidance.js` instances the Blender-baked members and changes only the LED panel's brightness phase. `worldtext.SignSheet` reserves tactical capacity in the existing atlas; lane-only vertex backlighting keeps the route legend readable at night without lighting other signs. Cached-world replacement releases the previous atlas texture, and battle teardown releases the furniture buffers. `lane_review.py` saves the editable destination-mesh review with packed textures; the production road review covers overhead, approach, night, bridge and tunnel views. `test/laneGuidance.mjs` guards direction, identity, bounded resources and omission behavior. Review corrections are limited to three render passes; synthetic fixtures do not certify every external map.

The route legend reads “戰備道”. Compact member heights and placement headroom share `laneGuidanceSize.js`; labels retain their atlas aspect. All guidance stays outside the movement blocker registry. The road review sweeps each prop footprint with the production player resolver before and after adding the mesh, so a size change cannot silently alter passage.

Deck queries accept a height band. Standing chooses a reachable deck, ceilings choose the nearest overhead slab, and ballistic overlap tests each intersected deck layer. Interchange branches retain their common datum and open parapets at shared road nodes. Bridge supports avoid other carriageways.

Land-base access profiles settle in `terrain.carvePlatforms` before road rendering. Both cutting and filling use the same approach; it extends beneath the slab across height-grid triangles to avoid an interpolation gap at the platform edge. Water approaches are omitted.

Ground pavement and paint consume the settled platform footprint when resolving their presentation height. The overlay stays above slab markings without changing platform standing or collision heights. Review rays test actual rendered pavement along navigation paths and check roads crossing the base slab, rather than inferring visibility from successful movement alone.

The `audit:roads` package script executes production planning, standing, collision-related queries and terrain excavation sources. The `shot:roads` script assembles synthetic terrain, junction and sign cases with actual production builders, probes roads in both directions at 0.35 m intervals using the standing and collision methods, and exports geometry, texture images and traversal results. It requires an existing Playwright/Chrome runtime and a local Three.js module cache; no dependency is added.

Blender MCP executes `author.py` for the member and corner-profile bake and `preview.py` for review assembly and rendering. The review uses exact exported road meshes with sectioned synthetic terrain around buried structures. It includes junction variants, a lane-width transition, local guide signs and a readable traffic-plate library, with editable scenes, packed textures and PNG renders under the ignored `out/road_structure_review` directory. These synthetic fixtures verify selected landforms and junctions; they do not certify every external map. Name tests also consume the captured Taipei and Shibuya OSM fixtures.
